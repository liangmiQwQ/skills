# Teaching decisions

## Starting from familiar sounds

Ask what is already recognized, writable and usable only when the saved record does not answer it. Use an existing self-report as a provisional starting point, not a placement-test result. Anime exposure may provide familiar sounds without reliable spelling, grammar or register.

Build each unit around something the learner can understand or do: identify an object, check whether someone is OK, ask someone to wait, read a label, identify an owner, express a preference or describe a habit. Use natural short exchanges and real sentences. Interest should come from understanding familiar language and being able to use it; avoid invented childish stories or disconnected vocabulary lists.

For a useful expression, connect:

1. Its ordinary written form, with kana readings for unfamiliar kanji.
2. The sound details that matter: marks, small kana, long vowels and rhythm.
3. Its meaning in the particular situation, rather than one universal subtitle translation.
4. Its register and relationship between speakers; label casual, polite and blunt forms.

Do not invent anime quotations or their provenance. For a specific remembered quote, request the exact line or verify an authorized source when spelling or meaning is uncertain. Distinguish an authentic quote from an original teaching example.

## Progression after most hiragana are recognized

This is an adaptable example, not a prerequisite checklist:

- Reuse hiragana in これ、ほん、なんですか and short identification questions.
- Explain dakuten and handakuten through familiar words such as まんが and だいじょうぶ. Marks belong to spelling; they are not optional emphasis. Introduce compound sound changes later when needed by an actual word.
- Compare small ゃゅょ, small っ and long vowels through ちょっと、まって、ありがとう、だいじょうぶ、かわいい. Count beats; do not equate small っ with an audible tsu or a silent pause in every consonant context.
- Introduce katakana through アニメ、ゲーム、ノート、ペン、コーヒー. Teach ー and the same sound-mark rules. Use the whole word while distinguishing シ/ツ and ソ/ン. Katakana is not restricted exclusively to foreign words.
- Teach の and も through possession and "too", with speaker-relative これ/それ/あれ.
- Use がすきです for preferences and simple i-adjective descriptions. Do not describe すき as a verb or apply じゃないです directly to all adjectives.
- Introduce useful verbs and を/で through reading and watching. Teach specific dictionary/polite pairs without deriving every verb conjugation from two examples.

Do not require perfect kana memorization before sentences or katakana. Introduce kanji with readings when useful, without silently adding a kanji-writing requirement. Treat handwriting forms and print variants explicitly, especially さ/き; a printed font is not a stroke-order model.

## Practice that provides evidence

Prefer: read for meaning, identify a relevant shape, cover and retrieve a phrase, change one meaningful part, then produce a true sentence. Give enough room to write. Two classmates can alternate questions and answers; provide a solo version.

Reuse the same vocabulary across different days and grammatical functions. Review a previously missed distinction in the next session. A useful amount of practice is small enough to correct and revisit; avoid copying every answer repeatedly.

Provide kana-first reading with optional romaji support that can be covered. Romaji is a bridge, not a separate prerequisite. State whether romanization follows kana spelling or pronunciation, especially ou/oo and ei/ee. Avoid Chinese sound-alike spellings as the primary pronunciation model.

## Feedback

Record the prompt, learner answer, expected answer or acceptable alternatives, result and explanation for each reviewed exercise. Separate missing answers from incorrect ones. Keep total scores optional; when scored, state the denominator and grading basis. For open writing, accept natural alternatives and explain context-dependent readings.

Distinguish visual recognition from unaided handwriting and audio comprehension. Paper exercises do not establish pronunciation or listening ability. Suggest a targeted next task based on observed mistakes; do not manufacture a precise proficiency level or imply completion from an artifact being downloaded.
